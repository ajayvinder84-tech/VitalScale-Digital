Why Public EV Chargers Fail, and How to Keep Yours Working

By Ajay Singh, VitalScale Digital

Quick answer: Most public charger problems are simple: a software or network glitch, a damaged or dirty plug, a tripped safety switch, or a payment app issue. Many can be fixed with a remote reset. The bigger problem is that nobody is checking, so a broken charger can sit for weeks while drivers leave bad reviews.

How common are broken chargers?

Drivers report broken chargers all the time on PlugShare and Google reviews. An Arcadis analysis of PlugShare data covered 5,794 public charger sites across Australia and highlighted how widespread maintenance problems are. For a business, every failed charge is a customer who might not come back.

A simple maintenance routine

  1. Weekly: check the charger shows "available" in the app, and look at new reviews.
  2. Monthly: visit, wipe the screen and plug, check the cable for cuts and kinks, check signs and bay paint.
  3. Every 6 to 12 months: have a licensed electrician do a safety test, and keep the report.
  4. Every fault: log it, try a remote reset, and if that fails raise a warranty claim the same day.

Who is responsible?

The charger owner is. The app company usually only handles payments, and the installer only steps in when you call them. That's why many chargers sit broken: everyone assumes someone else is watching. A care plan puts one person in charge of checking, reporting and following up.

Common questions

Why is my EV charger showing offline?

Usually a network or software glitch. A remote reset through the charger software fixes many of these. If it keeps happening, check the site's internet or mobile signal.

How often should a commercial EV charger be serviced?

A quick check monthly, and a safety test by a licensed electrician every 6 to 12 months, or as the manufacturer recommends.

Who fixes a broken public EV charger?

The owner arranges it, usually through the supplier or installer under warranty. Damage is often an insurance claim.
