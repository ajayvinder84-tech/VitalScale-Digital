How to start an NDIS business in Australia: an honest guide for 2026–27

A step-by-step guide from a Registered Nurse: the business basics, registered or unregistered, the rule changes coming in 2027, getting paid, and finding your first participant.

By Ajay Singh, Registered NurseChecked against official sources on 27 October 20267 min read

Quick answer: Get an ABN, choose the supports you'll offer, and check whether those supports must be registered. For most supports you can start unregistered and work with plan-managed and self-managed participants. Registration takes months and an audit you pay for, and more supports will have to be registered from 1 July 2027.

First, be honest with yourself

A lot of my friends and colleagues tell me they're thinking of starting an NDIS business. Here's my honest answer: NDIS isn't easy money. It's a business like any other, with a lot of effort, plus compliance, audits, admin, a team and complaints. And the people you support are trusting you with their daily lives.

An industry survey of 290 disability organisations found 48% made a loss in 2024–25. If you're after something easy, this isn't it. If you genuinely want to help people in your community and you can handle the compliance, read on.

Step 1: Decide what you'll offer, and to whom

Start with the supports you're qualified and experienced to deliver, and the people and area you'll serve. This decides almost everything that follows: whether you must register, which audit you'll need, what insurance you need and what you can charge.

It helps a lot if you've already worked in the sector, as a support worker, a support coordinator or a nurse. You already know what you're going to see.

Step 2: Set up the business basics

  • ABN. It's free from the Australian Business Register, and you can apply for an ABN and a business name together through the Business Registration Service. You also need an ABN to register with the NDIS Commission.
  • Business name. Register one unless you trade under your own name. A registered name doesn't give you exclusive rights to it; that needs a trade mark.
  • Insurance. The NDIS Practice Standards expect registered providers to have appropriate insurance, including professional indemnity, public liability and accident insurance. There's no set minimum, so get professional advice on the type and amount.

Step 3: Registered or unregistered?

For most supports, you don't have to register on day one. Who you can work with depends on how each participant's plan is managed:

  • NDIA-managed: registered providers only.
  • Plan-managed: registered or unregistered providers. You invoice the plan manager.
  • Self-managed: registered or unregistered providers, except for specialist disability accommodation (SDA), supported independent living (SIL), specialist behaviour support and supports involving restrictive practices. You invoice the participant.

The rules still apply if you're unregistered. The NDIS Code of Conduct applies to every provider and their workers, and you need an effective way to handle complaints.

What's changing: it's all getting stricter

  • Since 1 July 2026, SIL and NDIS digital platform providers must be registered. Unregistered SIL providers who were already running had until 1 October 2026 to apply, or stop.
  • Delivering a support that needs registration, without being registered, is now a criminal offence.
  • From 1 July 2027, the Government plans to expand mandatory registration to high-risk supports, such as personal care, daily living supports and supports in closed settings. The final list hasn't been published yet, and everyone in scope must be registered by December 2030.
  • Separately, from 1 July 2027 the vast majority of providers, registered or not, will need to enrol with the NDIA, with providers in scope enrolled by December 2027.

In my view, the Government is pushing everyone towards registration. Plan for it, even if you start unregistered.

Step 4: If you register, here's how it works

You register with the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission. Before you apply, you'll need:

  • an ABN
  • evidence that you can deliver the supports you're applying for
  • worker screening clearances for anyone in a risk-assessed role, including key personnel
  • a myID account to log in

Since 12 October 2026, new applications start in the new NDIS Commission Portal. The steps:

  1. Part 1: choose your registration groups, complete a self-assessment against the NDIS Practice Standards with evidence, and answer the suitability questions.
  2. Initial assessment by the Commission, then Part 2 of the application.
  3. Audit: you engage an approved quality auditor. A verification audit is a document review for lower-risk supports. A certification audit is for higher-risk supports and adds site visits and interviews.
  4. Decision: the Commission decides whether to register you.

Cost: there's no fee to register with the Commission, but you pay the auditor, and in my experience that can cost thousands of dollars. The Commission recommends getting more than one quote.

Time: it takes months, and a lot depends on how quickly you engage an auditor. An application lapses if you don't update or submit it within 60 days.

Worker screening: each state and territory runs its own check, with its own fee and processing time. In Queensland, South Australia and Victoria, workers can't start in a risk-assessed role until they're cleared. No clearance, no start.

Step 5: Know what you can charge

The NDIS sets price limits for many supports in the NDIS Pricing Schedule 2026–27, in effect since 24 September 2026. For NDIA-managed and plan-managed participants, you can't charge more than the limit. Different price limits for registered and unregistered providers have been proposed from 1 January 2027, but that's still under consultation.

Step 6: Get set up to be paid, and keep good records

  • Registered providers claim through the NDIS provider portals, which you log in to with myID and Relationship Authorisation Manager (RAM). Logging in with PRODA ended in November 2025.
  • From 1 December 2026, claims must be made within 90 days of the support, down from 2 years. Since 15 October 2026, claims made more than 90 days after a support already get extra checks and can be held for up to 28 days.
  • Since 27 August 2026, providers must keep records of supports and payments for 7 years.

Your admin has to be tight from day one.

Step 7: The hardest part is your first participant

In my experience, getting your first participant is where a lot of new businesses stop. You're new, and you've got no track record. There are about 280,000 active NDIS providers, so you have to stand out.

  • Look credible online. Support coordinators I've spoken to check your website and your socials before they refer. A website won't bring you clients on its own, but a weak one can quietly lose you referrals.
  • Be easy to find. Set up a Google Business Profile and keep it up to date.
  • Show up. The providers I see doing well go to NDIS events, are active on LinkedIn and in their community, keep up with the changes, and actually improve from feedback and complaints.

Step 8: Don't drown in repetitive admin

Referrals, intake forms, service agreements, emails and follow-ups: a lot of NDIS admin is the same task again and again. Simple automation, and AI used carefully, can take much of it off your plate inside the systems you already use. Keep participant information confidential, and make sure a person always makes the final decision.

Quick checklist

  1. Be honest with yourself about the compliance and the workload.
  2. Choose your supports and who you'll serve.
  3. Get an ABN, a business name if you need one, and the right insurance.
  4. Check whether your supports must be registered, now and from July 2027.
  5. If you're registering: myID, worker screening, the NDIS Commission Portal, then an audit.
  6. Learn the price limits and claiming rules: 90 days to claim, 7 years of records.
  7. Build a credible online presence before you need referrals.

Common questions

Do I need to be registered to start an NDIS business?

Not for most supports. You can work with plan-managed and self-managed participants while unregistered. You must be registered to deliver SIL, run an NDIS digital platform, deliver certain other supports, or work with NDIA-managed participants. More high-risk supports are expected to need registration from 1 July 2027.

How much does NDIS registration cost?

The NDIS Commission doesn't charge a fee, but you pay an approved quality auditor. Prices vary, so get more than one quote.

How long does NDIS registration take?

Usually months. A lot depends on how quickly you engage an auditor and provide your evidence.
