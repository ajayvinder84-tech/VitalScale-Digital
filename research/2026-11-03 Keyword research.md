## Read this first
- **These are ranges, not exact numbers.** Google only shows bands because the Ads account has no active campaign. The bands are 10k–100k, 1k–10k, 100–1k, 10–100 and 0–10 searches a month. "No data" means too few searches for Google to report.
- **Bid** is Google's estimate of the top-of-page cost per click (low to high, AUD). A higher bid means the term has more commercial value, because advertisers will pay for people ready to buy.
- **Not every big NDIS term is a provider searching.** "ndis worker screening check" (10k–100k), "ndis providers near me" and "ndis website" (people looking for ndis.gov.au) come from participants, families and workers. They're left out of the targets below.
- Any content that uses NDIS facts must match `claude/master-brief.md` section 4, or be checked against ndiscommission.gov.au / ndis.gov.au first.

## The five findings that matter

1. **NDIS changes is the biggest open door.** "ndis changes" and "ndis reforms" each get 1k–10k searches a month, with **low competition** and cheap bids. "ndis changes 2026", "ndis legislation changes" and "ndis mandatory registration" get 100–1k each. VitalScale already produces a weekly NDIS changes summary, so this is the best SEO fit we have.
2. **The policy offer has real search demand.** "ndis practice standards" gets 1k–10k. "ndis policies and procedures", "ndis policies", "ndis audit", "ndis audit checklist" and "ndis compliance" get 100–1k each. Registration terms get 1k–10k.
3. **Social media and AI have almost no search demand.** "social media for ndis providers", "ndis social media marketing", "ai for ndis providers" and "chatgpt ndis" all show no data, and "ndis social media" shows 0–10. Providers don't google for these, so the offers sell through content, outreach and referrals. Providers search for the **outcome** instead: "how to get ndis clients" (100–1k), "ndis referrals" (100–1k) and "ndis marketing agency" (100–1k).
4. **Websites get more searches than social media.** "ndis website design" and "ndis web design" get 100–1k each, with bids of A$5–23.
5. **Local search is tiny.** Every city term tested ("ndis consultant melbourne/sydney/brisbane", "ndis marketing sydney", "ndis website design melbourne", "ndis registration consultant sydney" and others) got 10–100 or no data. NDIS providers search nationally. For VitalScale, the Google Business Profile is somewhere people check you're legitimate. It won't bring many leads on its own.

## Google Business Profile
- **Its job is trust, not discovery.** City searches are near zero. People will find VitalScale through social media, YouTube, outreach and referrals, then Google the name to check it's real. Reviews and a complete profile matter most.
- **Service area:** Australia-wide or by state, not a single suburb.
- **Categories:** pick the closest matches in the GBP category picker (for example "Marketing agency" plus "Website designer" and "Business management consultant"). Check the exact names when choosing.
- **Services list:** use the words people search for: "NDIS policies and procedures", "NDIS audit preparation", "NDIS Practice Standards support", "NDIS website design", "NDIS marketing and social media", "Safe AI for NDIS providers".
- **Description:** nurse-led, NDIS providers, policies and procedures, Practice Standards, websites, social media. Plain English. No outcome promises.
- **Posts:** reuse the weekly NDIS changes summary as a GBP post.
- **Reviews:** ask each happy client (Samira first, once she's comfortable) for a Google review.

## To verify before using
- **"ndis logo": 1k–10k.** Probably people looking for the NDIS logo or the "registered NDIS provider" logo for their website. A short guide could rank, but the NDIS Commission's rules on using the logo must be checked first.
- **"ndis evidence": 100–1k.** It's unclear whether these are participants (evidence for plans) or providers (audit evidence). Don't build around it yet.
- **"ndis cancellation policy": 100–1k.** It's unclear whether these are providers writing a policy or participants checking their rights. A plain-English guide could work if the facts are checked against NDIS pricing arrangements.
